webtools-http-budget

The call budget of the suspend HTTP stack: it grants permits under a provider's rate windows and lane shares, pauses on the server's signals and holds a hard window across a restart. A consumer calls the CallBudget port, or wraps a call in withPermit, and settles each Permit with what the answer said; WindowedCallBudget, the supplied budget, counts epoch-aligned fixed windows with a boundary guard, holds back a floor per lane, queues calls first come first served and fails one fast when its wait would pass maxWait, refunds and re-plans on a cancel or a release, pauses on Retry-After and reset hints, and saves a hard window write-ahead to a BudgetStore; CallBudget.Unlimited grants everything. RateSignalsReader.Standard and RetryAfter.parse read an answer, a BudgetObserver receives every event, and two test fixtures stand in for the store and the observer.

Lifecycles and rules

    
A budget's, a permit's and a waiting call's life, the hard window's store, and the rules the budget holds to.

    

      
A WindowedCallBudget's life the supplied budget

        
  1. WindowedCallBudget(windows) { … } checks every setting and throws IllegalArgumentException naming the property, never a value; one INFO line with the settings' numbers
  2. the first acquire, utilisation() or flush() restores the store's snapshot once: a stale period dropped, the current one merged, a stamp further ahead than maxRestoreSkew keeping its counts only
  3. acquire grants, queues or refuses; permits complete or release
  4. flush() writes the exact counts, and returns the store's failure when it cannot
  5. close(): every waiting call and every later acquire returns Closed("budget"); flush(), utilisation() and the permits keep working

        
close() writes nothing and never closes the store or the observer: call flush() first for the exact final write.

      
A permit's life Permit

        
  1. granted: its units are charged now; the budget holds no lock or lease for it
  2. complete(signals): the request was sent; a reading raises a local count, a throttle extends the pause, and the waiters are re-planned
  3. release(): it was never sent; the units are refunded and every waiter re-planned
  4. a second complete or release, or one after the other, is ignored

        
Both run under NonCancellable, so a cancelled coroutine still settles its permit; withPermit completes it as spent in a finally, and a permit never completed is simply spent.

      
A waiting call first come first served per lane

        
  1. queued: planned at the earliest slot that fits, never before its lane's last waiter, and its units reserved
  2. it waits on the close latch and its wake signal, at most one minute a time, then re-reads its plan under the lock
  3. a refund ahead of it, a release(), a server reading or a clock step re-plans it; a new wait past its maxWait fails it with BudgetExhausted
  4. granted at its slot, or Closed("budget") after close(), or its own cancellation: refunded, and the calls behind it re-planned

        
A wake is only a hint: every wake re-reads the plan under the lock, so a lost, stale or double wake costs one lock section.

      
The hard window and its store BudgetStore

        
  1. one restore, lazily; a failed one is a StoreFailed event, and acquire tries again after storeRetryInterval
  2. before a grant passes the persisted mark, a save write-ahead of persistAhead grants, under the lock and before the grant; at most three saves in one section
  3. a full hard window refuses until its boundary, raising HardStop once per period
  4. a failed or timed-out save never advances the mark; grants go on from the in-process count, and the next save waits storeRetryInterval

        
No save before a restore has succeeded, so a budget whose store cannot be read never overwrites what the store holds.

      
Rules the budget holds to

        
One lock, events after it. Every field is under one coroutine Mutex, held across a store call for at most storeTimeout; the events of a section reach the observer after the unlock, so an observer may call back into the budget.

        
Two clocks. The wall clock decides where in the provider's calendar now is (indices, boundaries, the guard, the snapshot's stamp); the time source measures every wait. A backward step across a boundary copies the counts it left into the current period; a forward jump is trusted.

        
The guard. Within boundaryGuard of a boundary a call is charged to both adjacent periods, so a provider whose clock differs by up to the guard never sees a period over its limit.

        
Lane floors are never lent. A share is ceil(share × limit) units, at least 1, held back from the other lanes; the default reserves 20 % for Lane.Interactive.

        
Nothing prints a secret. The budget receives RateSignals, never a response, and logs numbers and class names only; RetryAfter's failures never echo the value.

Guarantees

  

    
Failure model

      
  • acquire returns Result.failure(HttpFailure.BudgetExhausted(wait, reason, window)) when the call's wait would pass maxWait (Window, or Paused during a pause), when its lane has maxQueued waiters (QueueFull) or when it can never fit (CostExceedsLimit, wait infinite), charging nothing.
  • After close(), acquire and every waiting call return Closed("budget") as a value, never as a cancellation.
  • Thrown, not returned: IllegalArgumentException for a cost below 1, a negative maxWait or a setting out of range; the caller's own CancellationException, its wait refunded.
  • A store's failure never fails a grant: it is a StoreFailed event and a WARN line with the cause's class name; flush() returns it. An observer's Exception is logged and ignored; an Error propagates.
  • An Error a store throws is not a store failure: it propagates to the caller of the member that made the store call, raises no StoreFailed and grants nothing; the lock is released.
  • RetryAfter.parse returns Result.failure(IllegalArgumentException) with a constant message; a RateSignalsReader never throws for bad input.

    
Extension points

      
  • CallBudget, Permit, RateSignalsReader, BudgetStore, BudgetObserver — implement Experimental behind @OptIn(ExperimentalForInheritanceWebToolsApi::class); calling and passing them are Stable Core
  • BudgetEvent — subclass Experimental, the same marker; its seven leaves are final
  • CallBudget.Unlimited, RateSignalsReader.Standard, InMemoryBudgetStore, BudgetObserver.None — need no opt-in, and are the seams' worked examples Stable Core
  • WindowedCallBudget — final, built by its factory; its builder's twelve properties take a consumer's store, observer, timeSource and wallClock Stable Core
  • Lane takes any name; PauseSource and StoreOperation may gain constants, so a when keeps an else Stable Core

Compatibility & versioning

      
  • Ships in webtools-http 1.0.0: new, unreleased, not on Maven Central until the HTTP stack is complete; it moved no version.
  • Checked: the ABI dump api/webtools-http.api, 557 to 892 lines with the budget, by apiCheck in check; the common code compiles for js. The dump does not record @SubclassOptInRequired: dropping the gate from a port passes apiCheck.
  • BudgetSnapshot.CURRENT_VERSION is 1; a snapshot of a version the budget does not know is dropped whole.
  • Experimental seams may change in a minor release; Stable Core changes only in a major.

    
Thread safety

      
  • Every member of a CallBudget may be called concurrently from any thread and any number of coroutines.
  • WindowedCallBudget: all state under one coroutine Mutex, never held while the observer runs; close() takes no lock.
  • Values (Lane, RateWindow, RateSignals, the readings, the snapshots, the utilisations, the events) are immutable; Unlimited, Standard and None are stateless; InMemoryBudgetStore is safe to share.
  • Confined: a builder, reachable only inside its factory's lambda. A RateSignalsReader must be safe for concurrent calls; a BudgetStore must be cancellable.

    
Resources to close

      
  • CallBudget is AutoCloseable: close() is idempotent, non-suspending and never throws; the default and Unlimited do nothing.
  • WindowedCallBudget.close() ends every waiting call with Closed("budget") and writes nothing: flush() first. It never closes its store or its observer.
  • Skipped, nothing leaks: the budget owns no thread, scope or timer; only its waiting calls stay waiting for their slots.
  • A Permit is not AutoCloseable: complete() or release() it; one never completed is simply spent.

Clocks and persistence

      
  • Windows start at multiples of their period since the Unix epoch (UTC), so they meet the provider's own boundaries.
  • A crash over-counts a hard window by at most persistAhead − 1 while saves succeed and nothing written ahead is given back. Grants made while saves fail run unsaved, and a count a server reading or a backward step raised is saved only with the next grant or flush().
  • A clock set forward by mistake and left uncorrected can under-count a hard window; several processes sharing one store or one provider are invisible to a local budget.
